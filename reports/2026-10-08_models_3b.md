# Phase 3b model iteration: calibration, blend, per-competition, persistence (2026-10-08)

Author: modeler agent. Full harness report: `2026-10-08_backtest_3b.md` (season refit,
2019/20 to 2025/26 complete + 2026/27 partial). Predictions:
`data/processed/backtest_predictions_3b.parquet`. Monthly refit (`--refit month`, 3b.4) is not run
here; the evaluator runs it next.

## TL;DR

- **negbin's over-coverage is not bias. It comes from integer bounds.** negbin's predictive CDF is
  calibrated: its non-randomized PIT puts 9.8 / 19.9 / 30.0 / 50.6% of matches below 0.1 / 0.2 /
  0.3 / 0.5. Under its own distribution, the integer bounds should cover 73.2 / 82.6 / 91.7%; we
  observe 73.3 / 82.9 / 91.9%. For integer outcomes, the pinball-optimal integer bound of a
  calibrated distribution covers at least q. The excess is about half the probability mass at
  the bound, which is about 5.5% here. Forcing coverage to exactly q (a conformal shift) makes
  every calibrated variant *worse* on pinball: 1.404 rises to 1.412. The earlier read ("bounds
  are about 0.5 shot too low") is a granularity effect, not a bias.
- The real, fixable miscalibration is **per competition**. negbin under-covers the Scottish
  leagues (79.1% at q=0.8) and over-covers Segunda (85.3%).
- **Recommended candidate: `negbin_pit_comp`.** It recalibrates the NB CDF per competition (PIT
  recalibration, latest 2 training seasons, global fallback below 300 rows) and keeps the
  pinball-optimal integer bounds:
  - coverage at q=0.8 is at least 81.0% in every competition and at least 81.1% in every season;
    the spread across competitions is halved (sd 0.75pp vs 1.5pp);
  - pinball 1.406, the best of all calibrated models (0.006 better than every exact-coverage
    model; bootstrap CI excludes 0) and only 0.002 behind raw negbin;
  - best on 2026/27 to date (pinball 1.567, 82.2% coverage at q=0.8);
  - the model also gives the honest, recalibrated P(total >= N) for each bound
    (`bound_probabilities`). The product should show this alongside N ("at least 19 shots, 83%").
- If the ship rule stays "|coverage - q| <= 2pp", ship **`negbin_cal_comp`** instead: exact q,
  worst competition 78.4% at q=0.8, all competitions within 1.8pp at every q, pinball 1.413.
- `blend` (negbin + qgbm) gives nothing over `negbin_cal` (pinball 1.4124 vs 1.4124) and costs
  twice as much. Not recommended.
- Recency weighting of the calibration scores does not help (details below). The 2026/27
  shot-volume rise is a mean shift that a season refit cannot see. Monthly refit is the remedy.

### Design choices

- **Calibration rows.** As in qgbm, the latest training season (at least 3000 rows; earlier seasons
  are added if the latest is partial) is carved out of `train` by date. Prediction rows are never
  used. The `_comp` variants use the latest **two** such blocks. A single season gives each
  Scottish league only about 165 rows, below the 300-row Mondrian minimum, so those leagues
  always fell back to the global value. Two seasons give about 330.
- **Scores are out of sample.** The NB that produces calibration scores is fitted only on rows
  dated before the calibration window.
- **Refit after calibration (`refit_full=True`).** The NB is then refitted on all training rows,
  and the calibrated level is applied to the new CDF. This is approximately valid: the refit adds
  one or two seasons to an expanding 4 to 10-season window, so the coefficients and alpha barely
  move, and what the correction targets (tail shape per competition) is a property of the NB
  family, not of one fit. Empirically refit and no-refit are identical to 3 decimals (pinball
  1.4124 vs 1.4122; worst competition 76.1 vs 76.3). Refit is the default so that a monthly refit
  uses the latest matches in the NB.
- **Level shift vs additive shift (3b.1).** Conformal on the score e = 1 - F(y) moves the
  probability level at which each CDF is read, so the correction scales with each match's spread.
  The additive alternative is CQR on the interpolated continuous NB quantile, a constant number of
  shots. Backtest: 1.4124 vs 1.4123, equivalent. Level was kept as the natural choice for a
  distributional model.
- **PIT recalibration (`negbin_pit_comp`).** In each group, choose the level t_q at which the
  non-randomized PIT CDF of the calibration rows equals 1 - q (Czado, Gneiting & Held 2009). The
  bound is min{k : F(k) > t_q}. This calibrates the *distribution* (where the NB is miscalibrated
  for a competition, the level moves). It does not force integer coverage onto q. Coverage stays
  at least q, which is what "with confidence q, at least N shots" promises.
- **Blend (3b.2).** b_q = 0.5 * NB interpolated quantile + 0.5 * qgbm raw quantile, both fitted on
  the pre-calibration rows, then one CQR shift. The weight is fixed (the members have near-equal
  pinball, so there was no tuning). Calibration uses the whole held-out season, including the
  early-stopping half, which only picked an iteration count. This gives 1.4124 vs 1.4139 on the
  conf half only.

## Recommendation

**Ship candidate: `negbin_pit_comp`**, pending the evaluator's `--refit month` run (3b.4).

1. Its bounds hold with rate at least q overall, in every competition (minimum 81.0% at q=0.8,
   and at least q at every q in every competition), in every season, and in 2026/27 so far. This
   is the promise the product makes.
2. It has the best pinball among calibrated candidates, 0.006 better than any exact-coverage
   model with a CI excluding 0. It costs only 0.002 vs raw negbin, in exchange for fixing the
   Scottish leagues.
3. Its CDF is calibrated (mid-coverage 69.9 / 79.7 / 89.6), so it can report the true
   confidence of each bound: `model.bound_probabilities(rows)` gives recalibrated
   P(total >= lb_q), which is at least q by construction. Check on 2024/25 and 2025/26: mean
   stated P at q=0.8 was 82.7% / 82.6%, realised coverage 81.1% / 83.4%.

**Caveat for the evaluator.** The automatic verdict rule |cov - q| <= 2pp flags
`negbin_pit_comp` (and negbin) as "NOT calibrated" because they over-cover by about 2.6pp. With
integer bounds that rule rewards a pinball loss: hitting q exactly requires moving some
bounds above the pinball-optimal integer. A granularity-aware check is strict coverage <= q <=
coverage, or mid-coverage close to q, or a PIT histogram. If the 2pp rule must stand, ship
`negbin_cal_comp`: exact q, all competitions within 1.8pp, pinball 1.413.

Not recommended: `blend` (no gain over negbin_cal, about 2x runtime, two model families to
maintain), Mondrian with a 1-season window, recency weighting.

## Persistence (3b.5)

`python -m shots.models.train --model negbin_pit_comp` trained on 94,678 rows (2010-08-06 to
2026-10-05) in 37 s and wrote `models_store/negbin_pit_comp_2026-10-05/` (`model.joblib`,
`metadata.json`). Calibration window: 2024/25, 2025/26 and 2026/27 (the partial season plus
2025/26 form the first block). Coverage on that window: 73.0 / 82.5 / 91.7%. joblib comes in via
scikit-learn. It is not pinned in `requirements.txt`, which is outside the modeler's write scope.

## Tests

`tests/test_models.py` is parametrized over the registry, so the new models get the contract,
target-tampering and target-less-rows tests automatically. The new file
`tests/test_models_calibration.py` covers:

- weighted vs unweighted conformal quantile, recency weighting and Mondrian fallback;
- PIT level and PitMap identity on calibrated data;
- the interpolated quantile against the integer bound, and the calibration window being carved
  from train only;
- exact-conformal coverage on synthetic data;
- the PIT model's validity;
- correction of an injected level shift;
- calibrated probabilities matching realised coverage;
- the save/load round trip, which gives identical predictions for all 8 models;
- the train CLI respecting `--until`.

86 tests pass (47 before).
